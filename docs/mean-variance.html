Robust mean–variance on a dataset

Take daily returns and the loss ℓ = −return. An adversary reweights the days, staying within a KL ball of radius ρ around the data, to maximise the mean–variance risk minus a KL penalty: J = sup { Eνℓ + γ Varνℓ − τ·KL(ν | data) : KL ≤ ρ }. Because Var = E ℓ² − (E ℓ)² is concave in ν, this is exactly the problem of the spin-glass page, and its dual has two parameters: q, the worst-case mean loss, and m, the Parisi parameter (the KL multiplier is τ/m).

How the adversary reweights the days

Likelihood ratio n·ν*(day) against the loss of that day. Days above 1 are up-weighted. The variance term makes the adversary push weight into both tails, big losses and big gains.

The dual: minimise over m

τ·P along the KKT path (q solves q = Eν[ℓ] for each m), on a log scale in m. Its minimum is the robust objective J, found by a one-dimensional search instead of an n-dimensional one.

Complementary slackness

KL of the Gibbs reweighting νq*(m),m. The optimum is where it crosses ρ, or m = 1 if it never does.
